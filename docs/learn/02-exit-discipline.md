# Exit discipline — planned exits versus improvised ones

Every closed trade in the Arena records HOW it closed: take-profit, stop-loss,
trailing stop, manual close, or liquidation. That one field, across enough
trades, is a portrait of your discipline.

## Why planned exits matter

A take-profit or stop-loss decided at entry is a decision made calm. A manual
close is a decision made watching the number move — sometimes right, often
late. The discipline read on your Arena account compares them: your win rate
on planned exits versus improvised ones, and your liquidation rate. It calls
the pattern *planned*, *mixed*, or *improvised* — a read, never a verdict.

## Trailing stops: paying for certainty with distance

A trailing stop ratchets your stop level toward the price as it moves your
way, and never loosens. You give up some profit at the top — the trail fires
below the peak by its whole distance — in exchange for never riding a winner
all the way back down. The Arena's trail only tightens on marks it actually
observed, and fills at the trigger price like every other stop.

## Reward-to-risk before entry

The ticket shows R:R — the distance to your take-profit divided by the
distance to your stop. A trade that risks 2 to make 1 needs to win twice as
often as it loses just to break even. There is nothing wrong with a low R:R
trade — there is something wrong with not knowing you are making one.

## The diary question

After each close, one honest line: *did I exit the way I planned at entry?*
If the answer is often "no", the fix is rarely a better plan — it is a
smaller size, so the plan becomes bearable to follow.

*Education, not investment advice.*

## Self-check

1. What separates a planned exit from an improvised one?
- [x] The exit level existed before the position did
- [ ] The exit was profitable
- [ ] The exit used a market order

2. Moving a stop further away after entry usually means:
- [ ] The analysis improved
- [x] The loss you accepted beforehand is being renegotiated under pressure
- [ ] Volatility dropped

3. The honest measure of exit discipline is:
- [x] How often your real exits match the ones you planned
- [ ] How often your exits made money
- [ ] How fast you exit
