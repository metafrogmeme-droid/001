# Fixes and upgrades — 2026-09-01

Five defects and two infrastructure changes from the v12-serving / v13-training
session. Four of the five were **silent**: the code ran, returned, and reported
success while doing nothing or the wrong thing. None of them raised.

That is the common thread and the reason they survived so long. A crash gets
found. A guard that runs, recognises nothing, and reports success does not.

| # | What | Where | Was it visible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SL/TP monitor skipped on ordinary ticks | `bot/core/engine.py` | No — no exception, no log |
| 2 | R:R guard blind to the model's real output | `bot/nlp/rr_honesty.py` | No — returned `None`, reported success |
| 3 | Eval parser graded correct calls as misses | `ollama/runeclaw_eval.py` | No — scored as a model failure |
| 4 | Test-suite flag pollution → red CI on `main` | `tests/conftest.py` | Yes, but blamed on the wrong change |
| 5 | Stay-awake claimed protection it did not have | `ollama/stay_awake.bat` | No — heartbeats kept printing |

## 1. Open positions went unwatched on ordinary ticks

**Severity: this is the one that can cost money.**

`_check_open_positions` is the stop-loss / take-profit monitor. Its call site
carried the comment *"this is the SL/TP monitor on every tick"*. It was not.

On the normal path it runs **last**, after scan and analyze, and two entirely
ordinary outcomes reached the end of a tick without it:

```
scan (non-fatal) → if not signals: return       ← skipped the monitor
analyze (fatal)  → raises on its per-phase cap  ← skipped the monitor
...
positions        ← only reached when both of the above succeed
```

- **No signals** is a routine result, not an error. The scan read the market and
  nothing passed the filter — and the tick returned before watching any stops.
- **Analyze blowing its cap** re-raises by design (`fatal=True`), because a
  half-finished sweep must not stamp the freshness gate. That re-raise unwound
  the rest of the tick, monitor included.

Neither is a crash, so neither appeared as one. Both left money already at risk
unmonitored until the next cycle — and a persistently slow analyze phase leaves
it unmonitored for as long as the slowness lasts, which is precisely when an
exchange is struggling and a stop matters most.

A second comment asserted the invariant that made this look safe:

> `# Non-fatal by default: ... Positions were checked earlier in this same tick,`
> `# so a skipped scan costs only the chance of a new entry.`

They were not. Only the four early-return branches check positions first; the
full path checks them last. Both comments are now corrected in place rather
than deleted, so the next reader sees what was believed and why it was wrong.

**Fix.** `_tick_guarded` now runs `_backstop_position_monitor()` in a `finally`,
covering both branches (with and without the hard cap). `_check_open_positions`
sets `_positions_monitored_tick` **at its end**, so the flag means *the monitor
completed* rather than *the monitor was called* — it runs under a per-phase cap,
and a call that timed out monitored nothing. `_tick` clears the flag at the top
of every tick. The back-stop can therefore distinguish *already watched* from
*never got there*; an absent check and a completed one must not look alike.

> **A wrong first attempt, kept here because the correction is the point.**
> The first version routed the five call sites through a `_monitor_positions()`
> helper. The CI gate caught four new failures: `test_tick_phase_caps.py` parses
> `_tick`'s **AST** to prove every await inside it is bounded and named, and the
> helper hid exactly that property from the test written to guarantee it.
> `test_scan_timeout_nonfatal.py` asserts the call site's literal source, and
> `test_scan_freshness_is_wired.py` drives `_tick` with a stub that had no such
> method. The indirection was the wrong shape; the property has to stay visible
> where the test reads it. Recording completion inside the monitor is both
> simpler and more truthful.

The back-stop is deliberately **non-fatal**: a back-stop that can itself take
the tick loop down is not one. It is never **silent**: reaching it means a tick
skipped its monitor, and that gets an audit line on the operator surface.

**Proven against clean `main`**, not argued: a tick that returned early made
**zero** position calls, `_backstop_position_monitor` did not exist, and the
no-signal branch never mentions `_check_open_positions`.

Tests: `tests/test_tick_position_backstop.py` (6) — early return, raised phase,
no double-check when the monitor already ran, back-stop failure not propagating,
`CancelledError` still propagating, and the hard-cap path.

## 2. The R:R guard could not read the model's actual output

`bot/nlp/rr_honesty.py` exists to catch a stated risk:reward that the text's own
levels contradict. On 2026-09-01 a live reply stated:

```
Entry | Stop Loss | Take Profit | R:R
$59,500 | $58,500 | $61,000 | 1:1.70

risk   = 59,500 − 58,500 = 1,000
reward = 61,000 − 59,500 = 1,500
R:R    = 1,500 / 1,000   = 1.50      ← not 1.70
```

The guard was in the path. It ran. `computed_ratio()` returned `None`,
`correct_stated_rr()` made zero corrections, and the wrong figure went out
unchanged. Nothing raised.

Two shapes it could not see — both of which are what the model actually writes:

- **A pipe table.** The label regexes look for a number *adjacent* to its label.
  A table puts every label in a header row and every value two lines down;
  adjacency is exactly what a table removes. Columns are now matched by
  **position**, the only thing that actually ties a header cell to a value cell.
- **Prose.** The connector was `\s*[:\s]\s*`, which reads `Entry: 59500` and
  `Entry 59500` and nothing else. `Entry at $59,500` fell straight through.

Plus the ratio itself written **label-last** (`for a 1:1.70 R:R`) or as a bare
cell under an `R:R` header — neither reachable by a label-first pattern.

### The part that nearly made it worse

The first fix **corrupted correct text**. v13's corpus teaches the honest derived
form `R:R = 10.00 / 5.00 = 2.00`, and the pattern grabbed the **numerator**,
rewriting correct arithmetic into nonsense. Silently corrupting a right answer is
a worse failure than missing a wrong one.

Two lookaheads, both load-bearing:

- `(?!\s*/)` — leaves a division alone. A separate pattern corrects the
  **quotient** and never the operands; when the operands disagree with the levels
  too, the line is left visibly inconsistent rather than rebuilt from numbers the
  function did not derive.
- `(?![\d.])` — stops the ratio matching a *prefix* of a longer number. Without
  it, backtracking finds `10` inside `10.00` the moment the first guard rejects
  the token, and the correction lands mid-number. **This is the third time an
  unanchored `(\d+\.?\d*)` in this repo has matched the leading digits of a
  longer number.**

Horizontal whitespace only (`[ \t]`, never `\s`) in both patterns: `\s*` crosses
newlines, which let a label in a table *header* bind to the first number of the
row below it and invent a claim nobody made.

The refusals are unchanged and now tested through the table path as well: a
missing column, a cell holding no number, and a header with no value row all read
as **unknown** rather than as a partial reading. It still never invents —
unreadable levels leave the text exactly as written.

Tests: `tests/test_rr_honesty.py`, 20 → **31**, including idempotence.

> **Note on v10.** This reply came from v10, which passed 22/22 risk checks and
> still misstated the ratio. The guard is the only thing between that and the
> reader — `RiskEngine` computes its own ratio from `TradeIdea` and never reads
> the prose, so this is an honesty fix, not a safety one.

## 3. The eval parser was wrong about the model

`ollama/runeclaw_eval.py` extracted `direction` only from a **labelled** line —
`Direction: LONG`, `Setup: SHORT`. v12 writes neither. It puts the call in the
headline:

```
TRADE IDEA: BTC/USDT LONG
```

The parser found no direction and graded a correct call as a miss. The yardstick
was wrong about the model, not the model wrong about the trade — the same drift
the `Risk Check:` comment in that same file already warns about, one field along.

**Fix.** A headline fallback, tried only when the labelled pattern finds nothing,
covering `TRADE IDEA: BTC/USDT LONG`, `TRADE IDEA [TI-4412] SOL/USDT SHORT`,
`TradeIdea: ETH/USDT LONG`, and the direction on the line beneath the header.

The header match is case-insensitive. **The direction token deliberately is
not.** Prose says "a long position" constantly, and a case-blind match would
harvest a call out of an explanation.

Tests: `tests/test_eval_headline_direction.py` (8). Against clean `main` the four
headline tests **fail** and the four *must-not-read* tests **pass** — so the
parser became more accurate, not merely looser.

## 4. Test-suite flag pollution — `main`'s CI was already red

`test_flag_status.py::test_default_on_guards_report_on` failed in a full run
while passing on its own. It reproduced on a **clean checkout with no changes**,
so the suite had been failing its own gate for a reason unrelated to whatever
change was being tested. `tests/known_failures.txt` is a deliberately empty
baseline, so any failure is a hard fail.

Cause: `BacktestEngine.__init__` forces `confidence_calibration_enabled`,
`setup_expectancy_enabled` and `external_sentiment_enabled` off **process-wide**
— correctly, and asserted on purpose by `test_backtest_flag_restore.py`
specifically so a later "fix" cannot make the restore tests vacuous. The override
is handed back by `run()`, `cleanup()` or `__del__`.

Around twenty tests construct an engine and reach **none** of those. pytest keeps
engines alive through fixtures and assertion tracebacks, so the release lands
unpredictably or not at all within the session.

Reproduced directly:

```
before ctor: True
after  ctor: False      ← the process-wide override
after  del : True       ← only GC put it back
```

**Fix.** An autouse fixture in `tests/conftest.py` restores the three flags after
every test. It restores rather than asserting, because a test that leaks one has
still tested what it meant to test — the leak is a cleanup defect, not a
behaviour claim. It cannot make the real behaviour tests vacuous: those set and
read the flags **within a single test**.

## 5. `stay_awake.bat` printed heartbeats while the machine slept

`SetThreadExecutionState` does not override the **lid-close** policy. On a laptop
set to Sleep, closing the lid suspends the machine while the script cheerfully
prints a heartbeat every five minutes. The old file mentioned this in a header
comment and then never checked — a confident negative, the same shape as an
unreadable price rendering as `+0.00%`.

It now **queries** the lid action (readable without admin; changing it needs one
elevated command, which the warning prints) and shouts when closing the lid will
suspend, hibernate or shut down the box. When the query itself fails it says the
action **COULD NOT BE READ** and to assume the worst, rather than staying quiet.

Two smaller fixes found by expanding the generated PowerShell and checking it
rather than trusting the batch escaping:

- `$ErrorActionPreference` was `SilentlyContinue` **globally**. A failed
  `Add-Type` would have produced a window indistinguishable from a working one
  while holding nothing at all. Now `Stop`, with only the optional `powercfg`
  query wrapped in its own `try/catch`.
- The `ES_CONTINUOUS | ES_SYSTEM_REQUIRED` literal is cast to `[uint32]`; as a
  bare literal PowerShell types it `Int64` against a `uint` P/Invoke.

It also reports how long it has held (`held for 31.5 h`), so a window that
silently restarted during a multi-day run is visible rather than assumed.

## Infrastructure — the 5090

### The 13× serving collapse

v12 (14B) serving **and** v13 (14B) training on one 24 GB card:

| | GB |
|---|---|
| v12 serving (8.4 weights + 6.3 KV @ 4 slots × 16K q8_0 + 0.3 graph) | 15.0 |
| v13 training | 14.8 |
| desktop | 1.4 |
| **claimed** | **31.2** |
| **card** | **24.0** |

`nvidia-smi` reported 23030 MiB physically resident against 29.8 GB claimed —
**~7 GB was paging to system RAM over PCIe on every forward pass.** Generation
fell from ~52 tok/s to **3.5–3.8 tok/s**; one 190-token reply took 49.7 s.

Two readings that looked fine and were not:

- **`ollama ps` said `100% GPU`.** That column reports Ollama's *layer-placement
  decision*, not where the bytes physically are. Windows WDDM handles
  oversubscription by evicting, not by failing the allocation, so Ollama's view
  stayed true and useless.
- **`utilization.gpu` said 97%.** That measures the fraction of time any kernel
  is resident, not useful throughput. A paging-bound GPU reads 97% while
  accomplishing very little.

**v12 cannot be made to fit at any Ollama setting.** Its weights alone (8.4) plus
training (14.8) plus desktop (1.4) come to 24.6 GB before a single KV byte —
cutting slots or context cannot help. Serving v10 (8B, 6.2 GB at 2 slots × 8K)
restored **50.34 tok/s**.

**A correction worth keeping.** The predicted `memory.used` after the switch was
~21000 MiB; it measured **22395** — barely 635 below the broken state, which by
that criterion would read as failure. PyTorch's caching allocator expanded into
the freed VRAM and does not hand it back, so occupancy stayed near the cap while
the thing that mattered — total *claims* fitting under the card — quietly fixed
itself. **`memory.used` was the wrong verdict; the eval rate was the right one.**

### cloudflared 2026.8.2 → 2026.8.3

Not urgent, and **not** to be done mid-run. 2026.8.3 shipped with no release
notes, no CVE, no advisory; 2026.8.2 is itself the fix release for the
trailing-slash and path-normalisation regressions in 8.0/8.1. The `WRN` is a
daily version poll, not a diagnosis — the tunnel held 4/4 connections for 24 h
with zero reconnects.

Restarting cloudflared on a **quick** tunnel mints a new URL, which means editing
`RUNECLAW_LLM_BASE_URL` on the bot host mid-training-run. Bundle the upgrade with
the next planned reboot. A **named** tunnel removes the churn permanently —
`serve_stack.bat` already auto-detects `%USERPROFILE%\.cloudflared\runeclaw-llm.yml`;
setup is in `scripts/cloudflared/README_LLM_5090.md`.
