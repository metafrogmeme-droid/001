Privacy

Last updated 22 October 2026

RUNECLAW is a web platform with accounts and a Telegram bot, which share data when you link them. The paper Arena also runs as an app inside the Pi Browser and inside Farcaster, signed in with that network’s account and opening a RUNECLAW account of its own. This describes what each one collects, where it is kept, and what leaves our servers. What you agree to by using it is in the terms of use.

The previous version of this page described a single-operator, self-hosted bot and had stopped being true of the product. Everything below was checked against the source rather than carried over.

What we collect

Depending on how you sign in:

  • Email and password — for email sign-up. The password is stored only as a bcrypt hash (cost 12); we never hold the password itself, and the confirmation field never leaves your browser.
  • A provider ID and avatar URL — if you sign in with Google, Discord or X. Accounts made by a sign-in that supplies no email (Telegram, X, a wallet, Pi or Farcaster) are given a synthesised placeholder address rather than a real one.
  • Your Pi uid, if you sign in with Pi in the Pi Browser. Pi gives each app its own uid for a person, and it is the one thing we keep from that sign-in: your Pi username is not stored. The Pi app offers it as a name for the leaderboard, and it is kept only if you choose it there.
  • Your Farcaster ID (fid), if you sign in with Farcaster. It is the one thing we keep from that sign-in.
  • A record of each Pi payment, a tip or Coach Plus, if you make one from the Pi app: the amount, Pi’s payment id and blockchain transaction id, whether it completed, and when.
  • How many questions you ask the Pi app’s coach each day, to keep its daily allowance. The count only: your questions and its answers are not stored, except that a question the prompt-injection check flags leaves a short excerpt of itself in the operator’s audit log.
  • Your Telegram ID, and username where Telegram supplies it, once you link the bot.
  • A wallet address, if you connect one. The signature proving you own it is verified in memory and never stored.
  • Two-factor secrets and backup codes, if you enable 2FA.
  • Your referral code, and who referred you.
  • Preferences you save — risk appetite, watchlist, interface defaults.
  • Which assets you ask the agent about — not the words you typed, but the ticker the bot itself resolved when it ran a tool for you, kept as a count per symbol over a rolling window of your twelve most recent. It is how the agent remembers you between conversations. Deleting your account deletes it.
  • Your trading activity on the platform — paper and live orders, decisions, and the audit record attached to them.
  • Your IP address, briefly — held in server memory for about fifteen minutes to rate-limit sign-ups and sign-ins. It is not written to any database table by the application.

Cookies

We set cookies, and the previous policy was wrong to say otherwise. They are functional only — there are no advertising, analytics or cross-site tracking cookies, and we do not sell or share data with third parties for marketing.

  • rc_auth, rc_session and rc_jwt — your sign-in session. The session token is HttpOnly, so page scripts cannot read it.
  • Your browser’s own localStorage may hold a referral code from a link you followed, so it still applies if you sign up on a later visit.
  • The Pi Browser and Farcaster apps set no cookie and store nothing in your browser. Their sign-in is a token held in the open page’s memory, gone when you close the app.

What leaves our servers

AI providers. When you chat with the agent or ask for analysis, your message is sent to a large-language-model provider. So is context the agent needs to answer usefully — which can include your open positions, your saved profile (your stated risk appetite and watchlist), and a one-line summary of which assets you have recently asked the agent about. In the Pi app, Ask the Claw sends your question with a summary of your practice positions, your recent practice trades and your Duel calls, read for that question and not kept. The previous policy said no user data reached these providers. That was not correct.

Which provider handles a given request is set by the operator’s configuration and can change; possible providers include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Alibaba/DashScope, and one configuration routes through a third-party relay rather than the vendor directly. We do not control, and do not here promise, what a provider does with data on its own side.

Exchanges. Market data requests carry no personal information. Orders placed on your behalf are made with your own API credentials and are attributable to you at the exchange.

Telegram, Google, Discord and X. Sign-in and messaging pass through their systems under their own privacy policies.

Pi Network. Signing in with Pi sends the access token Pi’s own SDK gives the app to Pi’s App Studio, which checks it and answers your uid. If you tip or buy Coach Plus, our server sends Pi’s payment id, and later the transaction id, to the Pi Platform to approve and complete the payment, and reads Pi’s record of it. A tip is a transfer on the Pi blockchain, which is public: neither we nor anyone else can delete it there. Pi handles all of this under its own privacy policy.

Farcaster. Signing in with Farcaster sends the signed sign-in message to Farcaster’s sign-in service, which checks the signature, under Farcaster’s own privacy policy.

Email. When the site emails you, the message goes out through Google Workspace from info@humanoid-traders.com, under Google’s own privacy policy. It emails you for the welcome and verification link, a password reset, and a notice when your password, your two-factor setting, your plan or your account changes. It sends no marketing email. Accounts made by a sign-in that supplies no email (Telegram, X, a wallet, Pi or Farcaster) hold a placeholder address, not a real one, and are never emailed.

Exchange API keys

This is the section the old policy got most wrong, so it is worth being exact. Keys are not kept only in an operator environment variable, and they are not held solely on your device.

  • Keys you submit are encrypted and stored on our servers, under a master key the operator holds.
  • The encryption is reversible by design — the bot must decrypt a key to place your order. This is not zero-knowledge, and nobody should describe it that way.
  • No interface ever returns a stored key. Status views show a non-reversible fingerprint or a yes/no.
  • You can remove your keys at any time from either surface. Removal rewrites the encrypted file rather than leaving the old ciphertext behind.
  • If you send keys to the bot in a Telegram message, that message is deleted on a best-effort basis. Telegram may still retain it. Prefer the website, and prefer exchange keys scoped to trading only, never withdrawal.

Wallets

Connecting a wallet proves you control an address and nothing more. The message you sign says so in its own text: it authorises no transaction and costs no gas. There is no code path in RUNECLAW that can sign a transaction with a wallet you link — every wallet route accepts an address and a signature and only ever verifies them.

The daily proof, and the chains it is written to

Every seal the site makes in a UTC day (among them the agent’s calls, Arena practice trades, Daily Duel rounds, calls, settlements and reviews of settlements, Memeclaw launches) goes into one Merkle root for that day, shown on /roots and in its public feed. A seal is a hash and names no one. A Duel call’s receipt, opened by its own seal, shows the call, its prices and outcome, the handle you chose to show, if any, and the chips you put on it, if any: play money that cannot be bought, sold or cashed out.

  • When your wallet anchors a day. You can write a day’s root to a public chain from your own wallet: to Base (a transaction to your own address, or a stamp in the Ethereum Attestation Service), to the DogeOS testnet, to Dogecoin from Dogecoin Core, or to Solana (a transaction carrying the root as a memo). Your wallet sends it; we never sign. Once the chain confirms it, we keep and show publicly which chain, the transaction id and the address that sent it. We do not record which account submitted it. The same facts are on the chain itself, where nobody, us included, can delete them; deleting your account does not remove them.
  • Bitcoin. Each day’s root is sent to public OpenTimestamps calendar servers, which put it into Bitcoin. What leaves our servers is a SHA-256 digest of one line naming the day and its root, and nothing about you.
  • Checking an anchor. To check one, our servers ask public blockchain services about its transaction id: RPC providers for Base and the DogeOS testnet, BlockCypher and SoChain for Dogecoin, public Solana RPC services for Solana, blockstream.info and mempool.space for Bitcoin, and, when the operator has configured it, QuickNode through a data service. They see a request from our server, not from you.

Memeclaw, the practice and testnet launchpad

Memeclaw (/memeclaw, and on its own domain when we run one, served by the same servers under this same policy) lets anyone launch and trade coins that have no value, in one of two modes. Neither needs an account.

Practice mode needs no wallet. Practice coins and practice DOGE exist on this site only.

  • What your browser keeps: a random practice key, in its local storage. Clearing the browser’s data loses it, and with it the practice player.
  • What we keep: a hash of that key (never the key itself), a random player id, its practice DOGE, the practice coins it holds, how many coins it launched today and when it last topped up. The practice coins launched (name, symbol, description) and every practice trade are kept under the creator’s or trader’s random id.
  • What is shown publicly: the practice coins, their names and descriptions, and their trades, each under a random id that names no person.
  • Forgetting it: “Forget my practice player” on the page deletes the key’s hash, the practice DOGE and the holdings. The coins and trades stay, under the random id.

Testnet mode runs on the DogeOS testnet. Your wallet sends every transaction; we never sign one and never hold a coin or a key.

  • What we keep when you plan a launch: the wallet address you launch from, and the name, symbol and description you typed, so the launch can be matched to them when the chain confirms it. A plan that is never launched is deleted after a day.
  • What we keep once the chain has it: the coin, its creator’s address, the launch transaction, and every buy and sell on its curve, with the trading wallet’s address. All of it is already public on the DogeOS testnet, and a launch’s terms are sealed into the day’s public proof, so they cannot be deleted from it.
  • What is shown publicly: the coins, their names and descriptions, creator and trader addresses, and trades, on the Memeclaw page and its receipts.
  • Your IP address limits how fast plans and trades can be asked for, as everywhere on the site.

Keeping and deleting your data

You can delete your account yourself. It asks for your password, your authenticator code if you have one enrolled, and the word DELETE typed out, because it cannot be undone.

Deletion clears the bot first and the website second, and if the bot does not confirm, nothing is deleted anywhere and you are told so. That order is deliberate: your exchange API keys live in the bot, and the failure worth preventing is a website that reports your account gone while the keys that move money are still held.

It removes your trades, positions, snapshots, alerts, watchlist, strategies, profile, diary, notification subscriptions, wallet links, the record of any Pi tips and Coach Plus payments, the count of your coach questions, and any exchange credentials on either side. Your sessions end immediately. A tip itself stays on the Pi blockchain, which no one can erase.

One row survives, with nothing in it that names you.

Your account row is kept as an empty shell — every identifying field cleared, the address replaced with a synthetic one. It stays because other people’s referral history points at it, and deleting it would take their standing down with yours. What remains is the fact that an id once existed. We would rather write that here than describe the deletion as total.

There is no automatic retention limit.

Nothing currently expires or purges account records, trade history or stored chat context on a schedule. Chat context is bounded by volume rather than by age. We are not going to write “we keep data only as long as necessary” over a system that keeps it indefinitely.

What you can do today, without asking anyone:

  • Remove your exchange API keys, from the website or the bot.
  • Unlink a wallet, or unlink Telegram.
  • Clear your saved profile — clearing it deletes the stored record rather than blanking it, and the change reaches the bot too.
  • Sign out everywhere. Logging out, or changing your password, ends every session on every device.
  • Export your closed trades as CSV for tax purposes.
  • Delete the account outright, as above.

Security

  • Passwords are bcrypt-hashed and never stored in plaintext.
  • Password-reset and email-verification links are stored only as a SHA-256 hash, so a database leak cannot be replayed against them. Reset links expire in 30 minutes, verification links in 24 hours.
  • Sign-in is rate-limited per IP and per email, and password reset never reveals whether an address has an account.
  • Two-factor authentication is optional, standard authenticator-app TOTP, compared in constant time. It is not prompted when you sign in with Google, Telegram, a wallet signature, Pi or Farcaster.
  • The application refuses to start if its session signing secret is missing or too short.

Children

RUNECLAW is not for anyone under 18. We do not currently verify age at sign-up, so this is a condition of use rather than something the product enforces.
